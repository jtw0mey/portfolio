    Project Overview

    Apple have pledged to make their products carbon neutral by 2030. To achieve this goal, they plan to reduce their 2015 gross emissions (38.4 million metric tons CO2e) by 75% by 2030. The remaining 25% of gross emissions (9.6 million metric tons CO2e) will be removed using carbon offsets, bringing net emissions to zero.

    In this project, I used data sourced from Apple's Environmental Progress Reports to visualise and evaluate their progress towards becoming fully carbon neutral by 2030.

    Key Findings

    • Greenhouse Gas Emissions (Ahead of Target): Apple are currently ahead of target in reducing their greenhouse gas emissions by 75% to 9m CO2e. As of 2022, they had already reduced emissions by 47% to 20m. Forecasting indicates that if current trends continue, Apple will reduce emissions by 86% down to 5m CO2e by 2030.
    • Carbon Offsetting (Behind Target): Apple are currently behind target in carbon offsetting 25% of their emissions. As of 2021, Apple had offset only 500k CO2e against their 9m target. However, carbon offsets can be fulfilled rapidly at any stage prior to 2030 through direct investments in environmental restoration projects globally.
    • Product Carbon Footprint (iPhone Trends): Apple's per-device carbon footprint presents a dual narrative:
      • Positive Trend: Apple have successfully reduced the average carbon footprint of their iPhones by 29% since 2017.
      • Broader Context: However, examining the full historical dataset reveals that recent iPhone models still carry a slightly larger carbon footprint than the 2015 baseline models.
